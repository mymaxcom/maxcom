MAXCOM R2, external camera guide

An external camera for live broadcasts

This guide is for musalla committee members. It explains how to set up a surveillance camera (a network camera) so that its picture can be used in a MAXCOM live broadcast, and how to manage its sound. Time needed: about 30 minutes the first time.

The labels below are those of the MAX Admin Client in English.

1. Settings on the camera

Open the camera's settings page on a computer (its address is in the camera manual). Set the following:

  1. Video codec: H.264 or H.265. H.264 is the simplest and the safest.
  2. Resolution: 1080p (1920 x 1080).
  3. Keyframe interval: every 2 seconds. If the camera asks for an "I-frame interval", set it to twice the frame rate (for example 50 if the camera runs at 25 frames a second).
  4. Fixed IP address: give the camera an address that does not change, for example 10.0.0.50, outside the range the router hands out to phones and computers. If the address changes, the camera will no longer be found.
  5. A view-only account: create one account just for MAXCOM that can only view, for example lihat ("view"). Do not give the camera's administrator account to MAXCOM.
  6. If the camera has its own microphone, turn its audio on and choose a common audio codec (AAC or G.711).

2. Check the camera stream on a computer first

Before using MAXCOM, make sure the camera really sends the right picture.

  1. On a computer on the same network as the camera, open a video player application.
  2. Choose the menu item for opening a network stream.
  3. Type the camera's stream address. It looks like rtsp://lihat:password@10.0.0.50:554/stream1. The last part of the address differs from camera to camera; see the camera manual.
  4. Press play. The picture should appear within a few seconds, smoothly, with sound if the camera has a microphone.
  5. If there is no picture, check the IP address, the account name and the password, and make sure the computer and the camera are on the same network.

Only when this step works, enter the same address in MAXCOM.

3. Broadcast sound

By default, the broadcast sound is the camera's own sound. The camera's picture and sound stay together, so they are always in step.

If the camera's sound is not clear enough (the camera is far from the speaker), press Add audio and choose an extra source:

  • This phone microphone, or This iPad microphone.
  • Mic-in on the box (a microphone plugged straight into the MAXCOM box).

The extra source is mixed into the camera's sound. It does not replace the camera's sound. If a source is not available, it shows in grey with the reason, for example "This box has no audio input".

4. The volume of each source

Each audio source has one row with a sound level meter and a Volume slider.

  1. Start with every source at normal volume.
  2. Ask someone to speak from the speaker's place.
  3. Watch the level meter. It should move when there is a voice, but not sit at full all the time.
  4. Turn down a source that is noisy or far away (often the camera microphone). Turn up the source closest to the speaker.
  5. If you want only the extra microphone's sound, turn the camera volume down to 0.
  6. Do not turn every source down to 0. The broadcast would be silent and the screen shows a warning.

The last volume settings are remembered for the next broadcast.

5. Aligned and Aligning

When the camera's sound and an extra sound are mixed, both must arrive at the same time. If not, every word sounds doubled, like an echo. MAXCOM measures the difference by itself, by listening to the same sound from two sources, and corrects it throughout the broadcast.

What you see on the extra source's row:

  • Aligning: MAXCOM is measuring. While the camera's sound can be heard, the extra source is not mixed in yet, so there is no doubled sound. Make sure there is a voice in the musalla.
  • Aligned: it is right. The extra source fades in and you do not need to do anything.

If the screen says automatic alignment has not worked yet:

  1. Make sure someone is speaking in the musalla and the camera can hear it. A camera microphone that is too far away or too noisy is hard to match.
  2. Wait half a minute. The search goes on and can succeed by itself.
  3. If it still fails, press Use <source> only, for example "Use This phone microphone only". The camera sound is turned down to 0 and only that source is used.

6. Sound from an iPad or Mic-in arrives 1 to 2 seconds late

When the sound of an iPad or of Mic-in on the box is used, the box has to line that sound up with the camera picture. As a result, the broadcast on the TV and on YouTube runs about 1 to 2 seconds behind what really happens in the musalla. This is normal and does not harm the broadcast. Do not be surprised if the TV in the musalla looks slightly behind the speaker.

7. Where to put the phone or iPad

If a phone or iPad microphone is in the sound mix:

  1. Put the device close to the speaker, for example on a table near the minbar, with the microphone facing the speaker.
  2. Keep it away from the loudspeakers so that it does not howl.
  3. Leave the device's screen on throughout the broadcast. If the app is closed or the device goes to sleep, the microphone sound stops.
  4. Make sure the device is charged and does not take calls during the broadcast.

8. Cameras without sound, and the Clap test

Some cameras have no microphone. Then there is no camera sound to match against, so MAXCOM uses the extra source only, and you set Audio time offset once, so that the sound meets the slightly late picture.

Clap test steps:

  1. Press Add audio and choose the microphone you will use.
  2. Press Clap test. The screen records about 5 seconds of picture and sound, then plays it back. During the recording, another person claps once in front of the camera.
  3. Move Audio time offset until the clap is seen and heard at the same moment, then press Save.

For an iPad the steps are the same, and you use earphones while checking. If the sound comes from the MAXCOM box, repeat the Clap test before every broadcast, because its delay can change from one broadcast to the next.

A camera with a microphone does not need this step.

9. External microphones

For clearer sound, use a microphone close to the speaker rather than relying on a camera microphone on the ceiling.

  • A microphone plugged into the MAXCOM box shows as Mic-in on the box, if that box has an audio input. A box without an audio input shows the reason.
  • The microphone of a phone or iPad placed close to the speaker (see section 7).
  • Some cameras take an external microphone directly. If so, plug the microphone into the camera and its sound becomes the camera's sound.

After changing microphones, check the Volume of each source again (section 4).

10. Recording to the camera's microSD card as a backup

Most cameras can record to their own microSD card. Turn this on as a backup:

  1. Put a suitable microSD card in the camera.
  2. On the camera's settings page, turn recording on and choose continuous or scheduled recording.
  3. Format the card once from the camera's settings page before use.
  4. Check the card every month: the date of the last recording must be recent and the card must not be full.

If the network drops or the broadcast stops, the full recording of the event is still on the card. Download the files from it through the camera's page on a computer.
